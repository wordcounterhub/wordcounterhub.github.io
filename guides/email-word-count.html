Email Word Count: How Long Should a Professional Email Be?

Short emails win

Most professional emails should be 50 to 200 words. That is long enough to explain the situation, make a clear request, and give the reader what they need to act, and short enough to be read fully on a phone between meetings. Studies of email response rates consistently find that emails of 50 to 125 words get the highest replies, because they respect the reader's time and make the next step obvious.

Long emails are not always wrong, but they need a reason. A project update, a proposal, or a complex explanation may need 300 to 500 words, and in those cases a clear structure with headings or bullet points keeps the message readable. What fails is the long email that buries its request in the third paragraph, forcing the reader to read everything before knowing what is wanted. Put the request first, then the context.

Structure beats length

A good work email follows a simple shape: one sentence of context, one sentence stating what you need, and the details required to act. If the details are long, attach a document or link to it rather than pasting it into the email body. Use short paragraphs, ideally one idea each, and bold the key date, deadline, or decision where it helps the reader scan. A subject line of 40 to 60 characters tells the reader what the email is about before they open it.

Cold outreach emails need to be shorter still, usually 50 to 100 words. The recipient has no relationship with you and no reason to read a long pitch, so the first two sentences must show that you understand their work and have something specific to offer. Personalise the opening line, keep the ask small, and end with a single clear call to action. One ask per email is a good rule; two asks split attention and reduce replies.

Editing emails down

Before sending, read your email and cut every sentence that does not help the reader act. Common filler includes apologies for bothering the reader, long introductions about yourself, and background the reader already knows. Replace phrases such as I am writing to let you know that with a direct statement. If the email is over 200 words, ask whether some of it belongs in a meeting, a document, or a follow-up message instead.

For newsletters, the rules differ: subscribers chose to hear from you, so 300 to 800 words can work well when the content is genuinely useful. Even there, a short summary at the top helps busy readers, and a single main topic per email performs better than five unrelated items. Whatever the length, test it with your own audience and watch open and reply rates rather than following a universal rule.

Putting It Into Practice

For one week, keep every work email under 150 words. Put the request in the first sentence, use short paragraphs, and move long detail into an attachment. Notice how much faster replies come and how rarely people ask for clarification. At the end of the week, keep the habit for routine messages and reserve longer emails for the few topics that genuinely need them. Short email is a skill that compounds: colleagues will start answering you first because your messages are easy to act on.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the ideal length for a work email?

50 to 200 words for most messages. Shorter emails get faster replies; longer ones need clear structure.

How long should a cold email be?

50 to 100 words, with a personalised opening and a single clear ask.

How long should a newsletter be?

300 to 800 words works well for most newsletters, with a summary at the top for scanners.
