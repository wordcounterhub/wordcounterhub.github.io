Character Limits for Social Media, Bios and Ads in 2026

Why character limits matter

Character limits are not just technical rules; they shape how people write. A 280-character post forces a single idea, a 150-character bio forces a choice about identity, and a 30-character ad headline forces a benefit into a handful of words. Writers who know the limits before drafting avoid the frustration of cutting a finished post down by a third.

Limits also affect how content performs. Posts that use the full allowance are not automatically better; on most platforms, shorter posts earn more engagement because they are easier to read while scrolling. The limit is a ceiling, not a target, and the best writers treat it as a constraint that sharpens the message rather than a space to fill.

Common limits across platforms

Post limits vary widely. Short-form platforms cap posts at a few hundred characters, while long-form platforms allow thousands of words, and the difference changes the kind of content that works. Bios are almost always short, typically 150 to 160 characters, which is why effective bios read like headlines rather than paragraphs. Comment limits are usually generous, but very long comments are rarely read in full.

Advertising limits are the strictest of all. Search ad headlines are often capped at 30 characters each, and descriptions at 90 characters, which means every word must earn its place. Social ad primary text allows more room, but only the first 125 characters or so show before a read more link, so the hook must come first. Email subject lines have no hard limit, yet most inboxes truncate them at around 50 to 60 characters on mobile.

Writing within the limit

Draft freely first, then edit down to the limit, rather than writing inside the limit from the start. Cutting forces choices: remove adjectives, replace phrases with single words, and delete anything the reader already knows. A character counter helps here, because it shows the exact count as you edit and removes the guesswork about whether a sentence fits.

Keep a cheat sheet of the limits you use most often, and check them quarterly, because platforms change limits without much notice. When a limit increases, resist the urge to write longer by default; the audience's attention span has not changed just because the platform allows more text.

Putting It Into Practice

Build yourself a small limits sheet for the platforms you use most, with post, bio, headline, and description limits in one place, and keep it where you draft content. Write each post freely first, then paste it into a character counter and edit down to the limit, cutting filler words before cutting ideas. Check the sheet every few months, because platforms change limits quietly and an outdated number can waste an afternoon. Writing inside a known limit soon becomes second nature, and your posts will get tighter even when no limit applies. Short, specific posts also travel better, because readers share what they can grasp in a glance and quote without editing. Treat every limit as a prompt to write more clearly, and your posts will improve even on platforms with no limit at all.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the character limit for a social media bio?

Most platforms allow 150 to 160 characters for bios, so write a short headline-style summary, not a paragraph.

How many characters show before read more in ads?

On many social platforms only about the first 125 characters of ad text show before the reader must expand it.

Do spaces count toward character limits?

Yes, on almost every platform spaces and punctuation count as characters, so count them when drafting.
