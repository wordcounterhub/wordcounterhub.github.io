Content Writing Word Count: How Much Do Clients Expect?

Standard lengths in content work

Most client blog posts run 800 to 1,500 words, which is long enough to be useful and short enough to produce efficiently. Product descriptions run 100 to 300 words, landing pages 300 to 800 words, and pillar guides or white papers 2,500 to 5,000 words. Social media captions are usually 50 to 150 words, and email newsletters 200 to 600 words. Knowing these norms helps you quote accurately and avoid agreeing to a 500-word price for a 2,000-word job.

Briefs sometimes specify a range rather than an exact count, such as 1,000 to 1,200 words. Treat the range as a commitment: delivering 800 words when the client expected 1,200 looks like an incomplete job, while delivering 1,800 words when they expected 1,200 can break their layout or budget. If the brief is vague, ask for the target length before starting, and confirm whether headings, captions, and calls to action count toward it.

Word count and pricing

Many freelance writers charge per word, with rates varying widely by niche, experience, and region. A general blog post might pay a few cents per word, while specialist technical or financial writing pays several times more. When quoting per word, remember that research, editing, and communication time are not captured by the count, so a short, research-heavy piece can take longer than a long, familiar one. Some writers prefer per-project pricing for this reason, using word count only as a scoping tool.

Clients sometimes ask for a higher word count believing it improves SEO, and it is worth explaining that quality and completeness matter more than the number. A 1,000-word article that answers the question well outperforms a 2,000-word article padded with repetition. Offering to cover the topic thoroughly at the length it needs, rather than to a round number, builds trust and leads to repeat work.

Scoping a brief by words

Before accepting a job, estimate the words each section needs. A how-to guide with five steps needs more words than a news update, because each step needs explanation and an example. A comparison article needs words for each product plus a summary table. If the client's word count does not fit the scope, say so early and suggest a realistic length or a narrower topic. This conversation is far easier before writing than after delivery.

Keep a record of how long your past articles took per 100 words by topic. This data lets you quote turnaround times honestly and spot topics where you are slow because of research. Over time, you will know your own speed for each type of work, and word count becomes a reliable planning tool rather than a source of stress.

Putting It Into Practice

Before accepting your next client brief, ask for the target word count and what it includes, then estimate the words each section needs and compare the two numbers. If they do not match, raise it with the client before writing, suggesting a realistic length or a narrower scope. Track your time per 100 words for different kinds of work so your quotes reflect reality. Writers who scope by words and time honestly earn better rates and fewer revisions than those who guess.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many words is a typical client blog post?

800 to 1,500 words is the most common range for client blog posts.

Do freelance writers charge per word?

Many do, though rates vary widely by niche and experience. Per-project pricing is also common.

Should I deliver exactly the requested word count?

Aim to land inside the requested range. Large misses in either direction look unprofessional.
