How Long Should a Blog Post Be? Finding Your Sweet Spot

Length follows the reader's question

The right length for a blog post is the length that answers the reader's question completely and no longer. A quick recipe variation needs 300 words, a beginner's guide to photography needs 2,000, and a detailed product comparison may need 3,000. Readers do not arrive with a word count in mind; they arrive with a question, and they leave when it is answered or when they decide it will not be.

Many new bloggers make two opposite mistakes: writing too little because they are afraid of boring the reader, or writing too much because they believe length equals quality. The first leaves readers searching elsewhere for the missing detail, and the second buries the answer under paragraphs of introduction. A good test is to read your draft as a stranger would and mark the exact sentence where the question is answered; everything before it should lead there efficiently, and everything after it should add genuine value.

What the data actually says

Surveys of blogging consistently find that posts around 1,000 to 2,000 words perform well for traffic and engagement, because they are long enough to be useful and short enough to finish. Posts under 300 words rarely rank or attract links, since they seldom cover a topic better than competing pages. Very long posts over 3,000 words can perform exceptionally well for competitive topics, but only when the extra length adds real depth rather than repetition.

Your own analytics matter more than industry averages. Compare your posts by length, time on page, and conversion, and look for patterns. If your 800-word posts consistently outperform your 2,500-word posts, your audience prefers concise writing on your topics. Different categories on the same blog often need different lengths, so set guidelines per topic rather than one rule for the whole site.

Practical length planning

Before writing, decide the post's job. A news update needs a few hundred words, a how-to guide needs steps with examples, and a thought-leadership piece needs argument and evidence. Outline the sections first and estimate words per section; this prevents the common pattern of a long introduction and a rushed ending. Write the draft, then edit with a reader's eye, cutting anything that does not help the post do its job.

Use formatting to make any length feel shorter. Subheadings every 200 to 300 words, short paragraphs, and a summary at the top all help scanners. A clear summary also serves readers who only need the answer, while the full post serves those who want depth. Both groups leave satisfied, which is the real goal of choosing a length.

Putting It Into Practice

Test length on your own blog instead of copying industry averages. Publish your next three posts at different lengths on similar topics, then compare time on page, scroll depth, and comments after a month. Note which length your readers actually finish and which one earns links or shares. Use those results to set a length guide for each category on your site, and revisit it twice a year as your audience changes. Your readers are the only authority that matters for your blog.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is 500 words enough for a blog post?

For simple news or quick tips, yes. For competitive informational topics, 1,000 words or more usually performs better.

Do readers actually finish long blog posts?

Most skim. Clear headings, summaries and short paragraphs help scanners find value even if they do not read every word.

How long should a first blog post be?

Aim for 800 to 1,200 words: long enough to be genuinely useful, short enough to keep quality high.
