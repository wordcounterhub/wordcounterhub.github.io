Novel Word Count: How Long Should Your Book Be?

Why genre sets the length

Novel word counts are set by genre conventions and reader expectations, not by rules. Readers of epic fantasy expect long books with room for world-building, while readers of romance expect a focused story that resolves in a few hundred pages. Agents and publishers use these conventions as a filter: a manuscript far outside the expected range signals that the writer may not know the market, even if the story itself is good.

Length also affects production. Longer books cost more to print, edit, and ship, and they take readers longer to finish, which affects reviews and word of mouth. A debut author faces the tightest expectations, because publishers take a smaller risk on a standard-length book. Established authors can publish longer books because their audience is already proven.

Typical counts by genre

Literary fiction usually runs 80,000 to 100,000 words, with 70,000 as a practical minimum for an adult novel. Romance typically runs 50,000 to 90,000 words, with category romance at the shorter end. Mystery and thriller novels commonly run 70,000 to 90,000 words, while science fiction runs 80,000 to 120,000 and epic fantasy 100,000 to 150,000 or more. Young adult novels usually run 50,000 to 80,000 words, middle-grade books 20,000 to 50,000, and picture books only 500 to 1,000 words.

Novellas occupy the space between short stories and novels, typically 20,000 to 50,000 words, and short stories run under 7,500 words, with flash fiction under 1,000. These categories matter for competitions and submissions, where the word count determines eligibility. Always check the specific publisher or competition guidelines, because ranges differ and a few thousand words can move a manuscript between categories.

Writing to length without padding

If your draft is short for its genre, look for missing depth rather than extra scenes. Subplots, character backstory, and setting detail add length honestly when they serve the story. A common gap in short drafts is a thin middle: the plot moves from setup to climax without complications, reversals, or quiet moments that let the reader care. Adding one meaningful complication usually adds both length and quality.

If your draft is too long, cut scenes that do not change anything. A scene should advance the plot, reveal character, or deepen the theme; if it does none of these, it can go. Many long drafts also suffer from repeated information, where the same fact is told in narration, dialogue, and thought. Trust the reader to remember, and say each important thing once, well.

Putting It Into Practice

If you are writing a novel, check the expected length for your genre before you plan the plot, and build an outline that fits it. A 90,000-word thriller needs a different structure from a 120,000-word fantasy epic, and knowing the target early stops the painful choice between cutting a third of the book or padding it. Track your word count after each writing session and compare it with your outline, so you can see whether the middle is expanding or the ending is rushed while there is still time to fix it.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many words is a typical novel?

Most adult novels are 70,000 to 100,000 words, though fantasy and historical fiction often run longer.

What is the minimum word count for a novel?

Generally around 50,000 words for an adult novel, though some genres accept 40,000. Below that, a work is usually a novella.

How long should a fantasy novel be?

Typically 100,000 to 120,000 words for a debut; epic fantasy series often exceed 150,000 words per book.
