Resume Word Count: How Long Should Your CV Be?

The one-page standard

For most job seekers, one page is the target. Hiring managers spend seconds on a first scan, and a single page forces you to choose your strongest achievements rather than listing everything you have ever done. Early-career applicants, recent graduates, and people changing fields should all aim for one page, because their most relevant experience fits comfortably there.

Two pages become acceptable once you have roughly ten years of relevant experience, a long publication list, or a senior role with many achievements to show. Three pages or more are reserved for academic CVs, medical professionals, and senior executives with extensive records. Even then, the first page must stand alone, because many readers never reach the second.

Words, not just pages

A one-page resume typically contains 400 to 600 words, depending on formatting, while a two-page resume contains 800 to 1,200 words. Counting words is a useful check when formatting makes page length misleading: a dense one-pager with tiny fonts may hold 900 words and read as a wall of text, while a spacious two-pager with 600 words may look empty. Aim for a comfortable density, with white space that lets the eye rest.

Applicant tracking systems also care about words, though in a different way. These systems scan for keywords from the job description, and a resume that is too short may miss the terms the system looks for. Including the right keywords naturally, in context, matters more than stuffing them in. Use the exact terms from the job posting where they honestly describe your experience, and keep the language simple enough for both software and humans to parse.

What to cut first

Cut anything older than ten to fifteen years unless it is directly relevant or prestigious. Remove the objective statement, since a short professional summary does the same job better, and delete references available on request, which waste a line every hiring manager already assumes. Hobbies and interests go unless they demonstrate a skill the job needs.

For each job, keep three to five bullet points focused on achievements with numbers: revenue grown, costs cut, users served, or time saved. A bullet that says what you did is weaker than one that says what changed because you did it. If a bullet has no result, either find the result or consider deleting the bullet. A shorter resume with strong evidence beats a longer one with vague duties.

Putting It Into Practice

Audit your resume this week: count the words, then cut one bullet from each job that states a duty without a result, and rewrite the rest to lead with achievements and numbers. Remove anything older than fifteen years unless it is directly relevant, and check that the first page stands alone as a complete pitch. Ask a friend to read it in thirty seconds and tell you what you do; if they cannot, the resume is still too long or too vague. A shorter, sharper resume opens more doors than a long one. Update the resume after every role or major project, while the numbers are fresh, rather than reconstructing them years later.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many words should a resume be?

About 400 to 600 words for one page, and 800 to 1,200 for two pages, with comfortable spacing.

Is a two-page resume acceptable?

Yes, once you have around ten years of relevant experience. Early-career applicants should stick to one page.

Do applicant tracking systems count words?

They scan for keywords rather than counting words, but a very short resume may miss terms the system expects.
