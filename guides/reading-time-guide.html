How Long Does It Take to Read? Reading Time Explained

Average reading speeds

Most adults read silently at 200 to 250 words per minute when reading ordinary prose such as novels, news articles, and emails. That means a 1,000-word article takes about four to five minutes, a 5,000-word essay takes twenty to twenty-five minutes, and a 300-page novel of roughly 90,000 words takes six to seven and a half hours of reading time. These are averages, and your own speed will vary with the material.

Technical or academic writing slows almost everyone down. Dense arguments, unfamiliar vocabulary, and the need to take notes can cut speed to 100 to 150 words per minute, so a journal article that looks short can still take an hour. Poetry, legal documents, and textbooks demand even slower reading, because every word carries weight and rereading is part of understanding. Speed reading courses promise 500 words per minute or more, but comprehension usually drops sharply at those speeds, especially for difficult material.

What changes your reading speed

Familiarity with the topic is the biggest factor. A doctor reads a medical paper far faster than a layperson, not because of better eyesight but because the vocabulary and arguments are already known. Purpose matters too: skimming for a single fact is fast, reading for a detailed summary is slower, and reading to memorise is slowest of all. Screen reading tends to be slightly slower than print, partly because of scrolling and distractions, and tiredness, poor lighting, and small fonts all add friction.

Your reading environment shapes speed as well. A quiet room with a comfortable chair supports sustained reading, while notifications, background conversation, and multitasking break concentration and force rereading. Short, frequent sessions often beat one long session, because attention fades after about 45 minutes for most people. Taking a brief break and returning fresh usually recovers more time than pushing through fatigue.

Estimating reading time for any text

To estimate reading time, divide the word count by your reading speed. If you do not know your speed, use 200 words per minute for general text and 150 for academic text. A 2,400-word blog post is therefore about twelve minutes of general reading, and a 10,000-word report is about 65 minutes at academic speed. Our reading time calculator on this site does this arithmetic for you and adjusts for slow, average, and fast readers.

Remember that estimates describe reading only, not study. If you need to understand, annotate, and remember a text, budget at least double the raw reading time. For a book club or exam, adding time for notes and discussion makes the estimate realistic instead of optimistic.

Putting It Into Practice

To find your own reading speed, time yourself reading a 1,000-word article you have not seen before, then divide 1,000 by the minutes taken. Repeat with a dense academic page and a light news article, and note the three numbers somewhere handy. With those personal speeds you can plan study sessions honestly, choosing how many pages to tackle in an evening instead of guessing. Revisit the test every few months, because speed improves with practice, and a realistic estimate is the difference between a study plan you keep and one you abandon.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many words per minute does the average adult read?

About 200 to 250 words per minute for ordinary prose, and 100 to 150 for dense academic or technical text.

How long does it take to read a 300-page book?

Roughly six to eight hours at average speed, depending on the words per page and the difficulty of the text.

Is reading on a screen slower than print?

Slightly, for most people, because of scrolling, glare and distractions, though the difference is small for short texts.
