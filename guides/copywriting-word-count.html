Copywriting Word Count: Short Copy That Sells

Long copy versus short copy

The old debate about long versus short copy has a simple answer: copy should be as long as it takes to make the sale and no longer. A low-priced impulse product needs a few lines, because the buyer decides quickly and extra text creates friction. An expensive, complex, or unfamiliar product needs longer copy, because the buyer needs reasons, reassurance, and answers to objections before committing. Testing, not dogma, decides which works for a given offer.

Long copy fails when it repeats itself or hides the offer. Short copy fails when it leaves the buyer with unanswered questions. The best copywriters write long first, covering every objection and benefit, then cut ruthlessly, keeping only what moves the reader toward the decision. The final length is a result of that process, not a target set in advance.

Lengths for common formats

Headlines should be short, typically 6 to 12 words, because they must be grasped in a glance. Subheadings can run a little longer, up to 15 words, since they carry more specific information. Product descriptions usually run 75 to 200 words for simple products and 200 to 400 for complex ones, with bullet points for features and a short paragraph for benefits. Landing pages vary enormously, from 300 words for a simple signup to 2,000 or more for a high-ticket offer.

Email subject lines work best at 40 to 60 characters, and the email itself at 50 to 200 words for most campaigns. Social media ad copy is usually 100 to 150 words at most, with the key message in the first sentence. These are starting points, and the only reliable guide is testing with your own audience and offer.

Writing tight copy

Cut every word that does not sell. Remove adjectives that state the obvious, replace passive constructions with active ones, and delete throat-clearing openings. A sentence such as we are proud to offer our customers the very best quality product can usually be replaced by a specific claim: handmade in small batches, or ships in 24 hours. Specifics persuade; superlatives do not.

Read copy aloud and cut anything you stumble over. Count the words, but judge by the effect: does each sentence make the reader want to read the next one? A word counter helps you hit a client's brief, but the real test is whether the copy moves a real reader to act. Test two lengths against each other whenever you can, and let the results, not habit, set your length guidelines.

Putting It Into Practice

Take a piece of copy you have written and cut it by a third without removing any fact, benefit, or call to action. Remove filler words, replace vague claims with specific ones, and delete any sentence that repeats the previous one. Read the shorter version aloud and compare its impact with the original; the tighter version almost always hits harder. Make this cut a routine step before any copy goes live, and test two lengths against each other whenever your platform allows it.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long should a landing page be?

It depends on the price and complexity: 300 words can work for a simple signup, while high-ticket offers may need 2,000 words or more.

How many words should a headline be?

Typically 6 to 12 words. A headline must be understood at a glance.

Is long copy or short copy better?

Neither is always better. Copy should be as long as it takes to answer objections and make the sale, and no longer.
