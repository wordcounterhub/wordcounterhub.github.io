Manuscript Word Count: Formatting, Submission and Editing

What is a standard manuscript page?

A standard manuscript page is a formatting convention, not a physical page. It means double-spaced text in a 12-point serif or monospaced font, usually Courier or Times New Roman, with one-inch margins on all sides. One such page holds about 250 words, which is why editors and agents talk about manuscripts in pages and words interchangeably. A 300-page manuscript is therefore roughly 75,000 words.

This convention exists so that editors can estimate length and reading time consistently, regardless of the author's software or printer. When a submission guideline asks for a manuscript of a certain page count, it means standard manuscript pages, and submitting single-spaced pages with narrow margins will look like an attempt to hide the true length. Format your manuscript to the standard before counting pages.

Preparing a submission

Most agents and publishers ask for a query letter plus the first chapters or pages, and the word count belongs in the query letter. State the exact count, rounded to the nearest hundred, and the genre, so the reader can judge fit immediately. A query that hides the word count, or gives only a page count in non-standard formatting, creates suspicion and extra work for the reader.

Check each publisher's guidelines carefully, because requirements differ. Some want the first three chapters, others the first fifty pages, and others the full manuscript. Some specify a font, others do not. Following the guidelines exactly is itself a test; a writer who cannot follow submission instructions is unlikely to follow editorial ones. Keep a copy of the guidelines with each submission so you can check you complied.

Editing a manuscript to length

If your manuscript is far over the expected length for its genre, the problem is usually structural rather than verbal. Look for subplots that do not resolve, characters who serve no purpose, and scenes that repeat the same beat. Cutting a whole subplot can remove ten thousand words and improve the book at the same time. Line editing, removing adverbs and filler, saves hundreds of words, not thousands, and cannot fix a structural problem.

If your manuscript is short, resist padding. Instead, look for places where the story moves too fast: a relationship that develops off-page, a journey described in one sentence, or a decision made without showing the reasoning. Expanding these moments adds length and depth together. A manuscript that is the right length because every scene earns its place is far stronger than one that hits a number through filler.

Putting It Into Practice

Format your manuscript to the standard before you count a single page: double-spaced, 12-point font, one-inch margins, and a header with your name and page number. Count the words with a reliable tool and state the rounded total in your query letter with the genre. Read three submission guidelines for your genre and follow each one exactly, noting where they differ. A clean, correctly formatted manuscript tells an agent you are professional before they read a word of the story. Save the formatted manuscript as a separate copy from your working draft, so later edits never disturb the submission version.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many words is a standard manuscript page?

About 250 words, using double-spaced 12-point text with one-inch margins.

Where do I put the word count in a query letter?

State the exact count, rounded to the nearest hundred, along with the genre, near the start of the letter.

How many words is a 300-page manuscript?

Roughly 75,000 words at the standard of 250 words per manuscript page.
