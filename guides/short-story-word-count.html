Short Story Word Count: Flash, Short and Novelette Explained

The categories

Short fiction is defined largely by length. Flash fiction runs under 1,000 words, often 500 or fewer, and tells a complete story in a single scene or image. Short stories typically run 1,000 to 7,500 words, with many magazines preferring 2,000 to 5,000. Novelettes run 7,500 to 17,500 words, and novellas 17,500 to 40,000 words. Above 40,000 words, a work is usually considered a novel, though some publishers set the line at 50,000.

These boundaries matter because competitions and magazines specify them. A story of 8,000 words is ineligible for a short story competition capped at 7,500, and a 1,200-word story may be too long for a flash fiction market capped at 1,000. Checking the word count before submitting saves the disappointment of an automatic rejection, and many editors reject over-length stories without reading them.

Choosing a length for your story

The story should determine the length, not the other way around. A single moment of realisation suits flash fiction, while a story with several scenes, a subplot, or a change over time needs the room of a short story. Forcing a big idea into flash length leaves it underdeveloped, and stretching a small idea into 7,000 words leaves it padded. Write the story first, then see which category it naturally falls into.

Many writers find that 3,000 to 4,000 words is a sweet spot: long enough for character and plot, short enough to hold in the mind while drafting. Longer stories of 6,000 to 7,000 words suit complex plots with multiple characters, but they demand careful structure to keep the middle from sagging. Whatever the length, every scene should change something, and the ending should feel earned rather than abrupt.

Editing short fiction to length

Cutting a story down is often the hardest part of writing it. Start by removing scenes that repeat information the reader already has, then tighten sentences, removing adverbs and filler phrases. Look for passages where you explain what the story already shows; trust the reader to understand without the explanation. If the story is too short, add a scene that complicates the main character's situation rather than adding description.

Read the story aloud to test its rhythm, since short fiction depends on sound and pacing as much as plot. A story that is too long usually feels slow in the middle, while a story that is too short usually feels rushed at the end. Revise until the length feels inevitable, as if the story could not be told in any other number of words.

Putting It Into Practice

Choose a length category before your next story and write toward it deliberately: flash under 1,000 words, a short story of 3,000 to 5,000, or a novelette if the idea needs more room. When the draft is done, check the count and revise toward the category, cutting scenes that repeat information or adding a complication if the middle sags. Then check three markets that publish that length and read their recent stories to calibrate your own. Writing to a real market makes the word count a creative constraint, not a chore. Keep a list of markets for each length you write, so every finished story has somewhere sensible to go next.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many words is a short story?

Typically 1,000 to 7,500 words. Flash fiction is under 1,000 words, and novelettes run 7,500 to 17,500.

What is flash fiction?

Very short fiction under 1,000 words, often 500 or fewer, telling a complete story in a single scene.

How long is a novella?

Usually 17,500 to 40,000 words, longer than a short story and shorter than a novel.
