SEO Word Count: How Long Should a Blog Post Be?

There is no magic number

Ask ten SEO experts for the ideal blog post length and you will get ten different answers, from 500 words to 3,000. The honest answer is that Google does not reward a specific word count; it rewards pages that satisfy the searcher. A reader looking for the capital of France wants one sentence, not 2,000 words, while someone researching how to start a business expects a thorough guide. Length should follow the question, not the other way around.

Studies of top-ranking pages often find that longer pages rank better on average, but that is correlation, not causation. Long pages tend to cover a topic more completely, attract more links, and keep readers on the page longer, and those signals help rankings. A long page that repeats itself, however, will underperform a short page that answers the question clearly. Word count is a proxy for completeness, never a substitute for it.

Matching length to search intent

Before writing, look at what already ranks for your target keyword. If the top results are 800-word how-to posts, a 4,000-word guide may be overkill and harder to keep focused; if they are detailed 2,500-word guides, a 400-word post will look thin by comparison. Informational queries usually need more words than transactional ones, because the searcher is learning rather than buying. News and simple definitions need the least, while tutorials, comparisons, and buying guides need the most.

A useful rule is to cover the topic as fully as the best competing page, then add something it lacks: a clearer example, a better table, a common mistake section, or an answer to a follow-up question. That extra value is what earns links and return visits. If you can add nothing new, a shorter, sharper post is better than a longer copy of what already exists.

Length, readability and engagement

Long posts need structure to stay readable. Short paragraphs, descriptive subheadings, bullet lists, and images break up the text and let scanners find what they need. A table of contents helps on posts over 1,500 words. Readability also matters for ranking indirectly: readers who bounce quickly send a weak engagement signal, while readers who stay, scroll, and click through send a strong one.

Track time on page and scroll depth for your own posts rather than chasing a universal word count. If readers regularly finish your 1,200-word posts but abandon your 3,000-word ones halfway, your audience prefers shorter content on those topics. Use your analytics to set length guidelines per category, and revisit them as your audience grows and your topics change.

Putting It Into Practice

Put this into practice on your next post: search your target keyword, open the top five results, and note their lengths and what each one covers that the others do not. Write your outline to match the best coverage, then add one genuinely new element, such as a worked example, a downloadable checklist, or a mistake to avoid. Draft the post, edit it for clarity, and check your analytics after a month to see how readers behaved. Length decisions made from your own data will beat any generic word count target you find online.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best word count for SEO?

There is no single best count. Match the top-ranking pages for your keyword and cover the topic completely, usually 1,000 to 2,500 words for competitive informational topics.

Do longer articles always rank better?

No. Longer pages rank better on average because they tend to be more complete, but a concise page that fully answers the query can outrank a padded one.

How long should a blog post be for beginners?

Start with 800 to 1,500 words for most topics. It is long enough to be useful and short enough to keep quality high.
